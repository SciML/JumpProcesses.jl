VRDirect and VRDirectFW

VR_Direct and VR_DirectFW simulate general VariableRateJumps in ODE and SDE problems with one exponential threshold for the total rate, as in a direct method. This page explains how they integrate the total rate over each solver step: with a piecewise Chebyshev interpolant, fit once per step, whose stored antiderivative answers every evaluation of the event condition. They differ only in how they store the rate and affect functions, so everything here applies to both, and both are called VR_Direct below. The code is in src/variable_rate.jl. The machinery shared with the other aggregators is described in How jumps are simulated.

At a glance

VR_Direct treats all $N$ variable-rate jumps as one stream of events: the next jump, of any type, happens when the integral of the total rate $\Lambda$ reaches an exponential threshold. It finds that time with a ContinuousCallback, so it runs alongside the ODE or SDE solver. The solver takes a step, and DiffEqBase evaluates the callback's condition over that step. If the condition changes sign, DiffEqBase finds the root, cuts the step there, and runs the callback's affect, which fires one jump. The first evaluation of the condition in a step fits $\Lambda$ over the whole step, and every evaluation uses that fit, without evaluating the rates again.

What is computed

With $N$ variable-rate jumps, the total rate is

\[\Lambda(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \lambda_i\big(u(t),\, p,\, t\big).\]

VR_Direct draws $E \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$ and fires a jump at the first time $\tau$ with $\int_{t_\ell}^{\tau} \Lambda(s)\,ds = E$, where $t_\ell$ is the time of its last jump, or the start time. Its callback's condition is the remaining threshold,

\[g(t) = E_{\mathrm{rem}} - \int_{t_0}^{t} \Lambda(s)\,ds,\]

where $t_0$ is the start of the current solver step and $E_{\mathrm{rem}}$ is what remains of $E$ at $t_0$. The condition starts positive and decreases, and the jump happens where it reaches zero. The rest of this page is about how the integral is computed, and how it is kept consistent across steps.

The pieces

1. Fitting one panel

fit_total_rate! fits the total rate on a panel $[a, b]$, which is the whole step or part of it. The points are the $n = 8$ (NUM_CHEB_NODES) Chebyshev points of the first kind, mapped onto the panel:

\[x_j = \cos\Big(\frac{\pi\,(j - \tfrac12)}{n}\Big), \quad j = 1, \dots, n, \qquad \tau_j = m + h\,x_j, \quad m = \tfrac{a+b}{2}, \quad h = \tfrac{b-a}{2}.\]

At each point, the state comes from the solver's interpolant, and every rate is evaluated (total_variable_rate):

\[f_j = \Lambda\big(u(\tau_j),\, p,\, \tau_j\big).\]

A matrix (transform) maps the samples to Chebyshev coefficients,

\[c_k = \frac{2}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} f_j \cos\Big(\frac{\pi k\,(j - \tfrac12)}{n}\Big), \qquad k = 0, \dots, n-1,\]

which define the interpolant on the panel, in the variable $x \in [-1, 1]$:

\[q(x) = \frac{c_0}{2} + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} c_k\, T_k(x), \qquad q(x_j) = f_j.\]

Its integral over the panel uses $\int_{-1}^{1} T_k\,dx = 2/(1-k^2)$ for even $k$ and $0$ for odd $k$ (the weights integral_weights):

\[I = h \Big( c_0 + \sum_{\substack{k = 2 \\ k \text{ even}}}^{n-1} \frac{2\, c_k}{1 - k^2} \Big).\]

The estimate of its error is the size of the last two coefficients,

\[\varepsilon = (b - a)\big(|c_{n-1}| + |c_{n-2}|\big),\]

for the reasons in A.1. The points, the matrix and the weights are computed once, with cospi in the problem's time type, eltype(prob.tspan), so a higher precision type such as BigFloat is not limited to Float64 accuracy.

2. Refining, and stopping at the jump

fit_step! fits the step $[t_0, t_1]$ with fit_panels!, recursively from left to right. Let $S(a)$ be the integral accumulated from $t_0$ to a panel's start, and $R(a) = E_{\mathrm{rem}} - S(a)$ the remaining threshold there. A panel's integral must be accurate to

\[\mathrm{tol} = \mathrm{abstol} + \mathrm{reltol}\,\big|R(a)\big|,\]

with the solver's abstol and reltol, or their smallest entries if they are arrays. This is the test the solver would apply if $R$ minus the integral were a component of the state, as each jump's integrated rate is with VR_FRM; see A.2.

The panel contains the jump if the total passes the threshold, $S(a) + I > E_{\mathrm{rem}}$. That total is the number stored for the panel's end and used by the condition there (§3, §4), so the fit and the condition agree under rounding. The comparison is strict, so that the condition is negative after the panel, rather than zero over the rest of the step. On that panel the interpolant must also be accurate relative to $R(a)$, which can be much smaller than $I$, so the rounding error of its antiderivative is added to the estimate (antiderivative_rounding; see A.3):

\[\varepsilon \leftarrow \max\Big(\varepsilon,\; (b - a)(n + 1)\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \sum_{k} |c_k| \Big).\]

Then:

if ε > tol and depth < D and a < m < b:   # D: MAX_CHEB_DEPTH = 12, or
    fit [a, m]                            #    MAX_CHEB_CROSSING_DEPTH = 40
    if it passed the threshold: stop      #    for the panel with the jump
    fit [m, b]
else:
    store the panel, with S(a) and S(b) = S(a) + I

Fitting stops after the panel where the threshold is passed, since the solver will cut the step at the jump. The test $a < m < b$ stops halving once a panel can't be split in floating point.

3. What each panel stores

Instead of $q$, push_panel! stores the coefficients of its antiderivative in $x$, chosen to be zero at $x = -1$:

\[\int_{-1}^{x} q(s)\,ds = \sum_{j=0}^{n} C_j\, T_j(x), \qquad C_j = \frac{c_{j-1} - c_{j+1}}{2j} \;\; (j \ge 1,\; c_k = 0 \text{ for } k \ge n), \qquad C_0 = -\sum_{j=1}^{n} (-1)^j\, C_j.\]

4. Evaluating the condition

For each evaluation, at the step's ends and during root finding, fitted_rate_integral finds the panel containing $t$ by binary search, or the last panel if $t$ is past it, and the condition returns

\[g(t) = \begin{cases} E_{\mathrm{rem}} - \Big[\, S(a) + h \displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n} C_j\, T_j(x) \Big], & x = \dfrac{2t - a - b}{b - a}, \quad t < b, \\[1.2em] E_{\mathrm{rem}} - S(b), & t \ge b, \end{cases}\]

with the sum computed by Clenshaw's recurrence (chebyshev_sum):

\[\beta_{n+1} = \beta_{n+2} = 0, \qquad \beta_j = C_j + 2x\,\beta_{j+1} - \beta_{j+2} \;\; (j = n, \dots, 1), \qquad \sum_{j=0}^{n} C_j T_j(x) = C_0 + x\,\beta_1 - \beta_2.\]

That is a few dozen floating-point operations, with no rate evaluations. At or past a panel's end, $g$ uses the stored total $S(b)$: the number the fit decided with, and the one the next panel starts from. Past the last panel it keeps that value instead of extrapolating the polynomial, and since the fit stops only once $S(b) > E_{\mathrm{rem}}$, the value there is negative.

5. Between steps

DiffEqBase evaluates the condition only while checking a step $[t_{\mathrm{prev}}, t]$: first at $t_{\mathrm{prev}}$, then at $t$ and during root finding. So the fit is current exactly when the step's two ends are those of the fitted step. The first evaluation for a new step, seen as a change in integrator.tprev or integrator.t, commits the previous fit $F_{\mathrm{old}}$ up to the new step's start, and fits the new step:

\[E_{\mathrm{rem}} \leftarrow E_{\mathrm{rem}} - F_{\mathrm{old}}(t_{\mathrm{prev}}), \qquad t_0 \leftarrow t_{\mathrm{prev}}, \qquad \text{then fit } [t_0,\, t].\]

  • After a normal step, $t_{\mathrm{prev}}$ is the old step's end, so this subtracts the whole previous integral.
  • If another callback's event cut the step short, the next step starts at the event, before the old fit's end. Only the integral up to the event is used, and the rest of the interval is fitted again with the state after the event. The next step can end where the cut step would have, for example at the final time, so the start is compared as well as the end.
  • While the integrator is still at $t_0$, as right after one of VR_Direct's own jumps, there is nothing to fit.

At initialization, $E_{\mathrm{rem}}$ is drawn, $t_0$ is the start time, and the fit is empty.

6. Firing a jump

At the root $\tau$, the affect evaluates every rate at $u(\tau)$, keeping their cumulative sums (cum_rate_sum). It draws $U\,\Lambda(\tau)$ with $U$ uniform on $[0, 1)$, finds the jump $i$ whose interval of the cumulative sums contains it, and runs that jump's affect. It then draws a new threshold, sets $t_0 = \tau$, and empties the fit.

Interaction with the integrator

  • When the condition is evaluated. The callback has interp_points = 0, so DiffEqBase checks only the step's ends before root finding. Interior checks are not needed, since the fit has already examined the whole step: the condition changes sign over the step exactly when the fit found the threshold reached in it. Root finding then evaluates only the fit.
  • Other events. The jumps of an SSA aggregator, user tstops, and discrete callbacks all act at the end of a step, so the next step's fit starts from the state after them, with no reset needed. A continuous callback's event can end a step early, which §5 handles.
  • Callback options. All the jumps share one ContinuousCallback. Its save_positions is the elementwise OR of the jumps' save_positions, and its abstol and reltol are the smallest of the jumps'. The jumps' rootfind and interp_points are not used.
  • SDEs. The rates are evaluated along the solver's interpolant between steps. In an SDE that doesn't resolve the noise within a step, so a rate that depends on a component driven by noise needs steps that are short compared with the rate's variation, for example by setting dtmax. VR_FRM integrates each rate as a component of the state instead, so the SDE solver's error control covers it.
  • Number types. The thresholds, the fit and its tables use the time type of the problem, eltype(prob.tspan).

Accuracy and error control

  • The solver's abstol and reltol control the state, and so the rates at the fit's points, through the solver's error control and interpolant. They also set the tolerance of each panel's integral (§2). An error $\delta$ in the integral moves a jump by about $\delta / \Lambda(\tau)$.
  • The jumps' abstol and reltol are passed to the callback. In current DiffEqBase versions, abstol only guards against detecting the same event twice, and root finding brackets the root of $g$ to adjacent floating-point times. They don't affect the accuracy of the integral.
  • The depth limits are fixed. A panel that reaches its limit is accepted even if its error estimate is above the tolerance.

The errors come from the solver's interpolant of the state; from interpolating $\Lambda$ on each panel, which the tail coefficients estimate (A.1); from rounding, which is bounded on the panel with the jump (A.3); and from root finding, which is accurate to the floating-point resolution of the time.

These are checked in test/variable_rate.jl. Test 7 compares first jump times with those from the analytically integrated rate, to a relative error of $10^{-5}$ in Float32 and $10^{-6}$ in Float64, with solver steps that each integrate to many times the threshold. Test 11 does so in BigFloat, to $10^{-25}$ with tolerances of $10^{-30}$. Test 12 checks thresholds reached exactly at panel ends. Tests 6 and 9 and the "VariableRateJump mean counts vs exact" tests check mean numbers of jumps, Test 8 a rate driven by SDE noise, and Tests 9 and 10 steps cut short by another callback.

Cost

For $N$ jumps and $n = 8$ points per panel:

WorkCost
fitting a step$n$ state interpolations and $nN$ rate evaluations per panel fitted
each evaluation of the conditiona binary search over the step's panels and a sum of $n + 1$ terms; no rate evaluations
firing a jump$N$ rate evaluations and a binary search
memory$n + 5 = 13$ numbers per panel of the current step

A smooth rate usually needs one panel per step. A halved panel's samples are not reused, since first-kind points don't nest, so a step that ends with $P$ panels costs at most $2P - 1$ panel fits. Root finding and the other condition evaluations add almost nothing, so the cost per step doesn't depend on how many there are.

Limitations, and when to use something else

  • All the rates at every point. Each point of the fit evaluates all $N$ rates, whatever they depend on, so the cost per step grows with $N$. When bounds on the rates are available, Coevolve evaluates each jump's rate only at that jump's own candidate times.
  • Rates driven by SDE noise need short steps, or VR_FRM; see above.
  • Features between the points. The error estimate sees only the samples. A feature that falls between a panel's points, such as a narrow pulse in a rate over a long step, or an oscillation at a frequency that aliases into small final coefficients, can be missed with no sign in the estimate. If a rate has such features at known times, pass those times as tstops, or limit the step size with dtmax.
  • Discontinuous rates. Near a discontinuity or a kink, the coefficients decay slowly, and panels are halved up to the depth limit. Passing the time of a known discontinuity as a tstop makes it a step boundary instead.

References

Sampling the next jump from one exponential threshold on the integrated total rate, and choosing the jump with probability $\lambda_i / \Lambda$, is Gillespie's direct method [1] applied to time-dependent rates. Locating the jump time with the solver's event detection follows the hybrid method of Salis and Kaznessis [2]. Chebyshev interpolation at first-kind points, its coefficients' decay, the aliasing bound and the integration of Chebyshev series are standard [3], as is Clenshaw's recurrence [4]. Specific to JumpProcesses are fitting the total rate once per solver step and answering every evaluation of the condition from the stored antiderivative, the tolerance relative to the remaining threshold, stopping the fit at the jump, and the rounding term.

Appendix: the error estimates

Both estimates are heuristics rather than rigorous bounds. The one in §1 estimates how far a panel's interpolant is from the true rate. The tolerance in §2 decides how small that error must be, with a rounding term added on the one panel where rounding can matter.

A.1 The interpolation estimate

Expand the true rate on the panel in Chebyshev polynomials:

\[\Lambda(x) = \sum_{k \ge 0} a_k\, T_k(x).\]

For a smooth rate the coefficients decay quickly: geometrically, $|a_k| \lesssim C\rho^{-k}$ with $\rho > 1$, when $\Lambda$ is analytic in a neighborhood of the panel, and algebraically when it is only finitely differentiable [3]. The interpolant of degree $n-1$ captures the terms up to $k = n-1$, so its error comes from the omitted tail, at most doubled by aliasing:

\[\max_{x \in [-1,1]} \big|\Lambda(x) - q(x)\big| \le 2 \sum_{k \ge n} |a_k|.\]

The tail is not known, but with geometric decay it is comparable to the last coefficients that are: for $\rho \ge 2$, $\sum_{k \ge n} |a_k| \lesssim |a_{n-1}|$. The computed $c_k$ are close to $a_k$ when the coefficients decay, so $|c_{n-1}|$ and $|c_{n-2}|$ stand in for the tail. Two coefficients are used rather than one because a rate that is even or odd about the panel's midpoint has every other coefficient zero, so $c_{n-1}$ alone could vanish while the error does not.

The factor $b - a$ turns an error in the rate into an error in the panel's integral:

\[\Big| \int_a^b (\Lambda - q)\, dt \Big| = h\, \Big| \int_{-1}^{1} (\Lambda - q)\, dx \Big| \le 2h \max_x |\Lambda - q| = (b - a) \max_x |\Lambda - q|.\]

For the integral this is usually pessimistic, since integration damps high-order error: $\int_{-1}^{1} T_k\,dx$ is $0$ for odd $k$ and $2/(1-k^2)$ for even $k$. It is not a guaranteed bound, though: a rate oscillating at just the wrong frequency for a panel's length can alias into small final coefficients.

A.2 The tolerance

\[\mathrm{tol} = \mathrm{abstol} + \mathrm{reltol}\,\big|R(a)\big|, \qquad R(a) = E_{\mathrm{rem}} - S(a).\]

This is the mixed test an ODE solver applies to a component $y$ of the state, $\mathrm{abstol} + \mathrm{reltol}\,|y|$, with $y$ the remaining threshold. There are two reasons for it:

  • With VR_FRM, each jump's integrated rate is a component of the state, so the solver's own error control governs it. Using the same form gives VR_Direct comparable accuracy for the same abstol and reltol.
  • It is the scale that sets the jump time. The jump happens where $R(a) - \int_a^{\tau} \Lambda = 0$, so an error $\delta$ in that integral moves it by about $\Delta\tau \approx \delta / \Lambda(\tau)$. Measuring $\delta$ against $|R|$, which is of order one since it comes from an $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ draw, controls the jump time on a consistent scale. A tolerance relative to the panel's integral $I$ instead would allow errors larger than $R$ itself when $I$ is much larger than $R$.

A.3 The rounding term

The condition evaluates $S(a) + h \sum_j C_j T_j(x)$ by Clenshaw's recurrence, whose absolute rounding error is about $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ times the number of terms times their size. Since $C_j = (c_{j-1} - c_{j+1})/(2j)$ and $|C_0| \le \sum_{j \ge 1} |C_j|$,

\[\sum_{j=0}^{n} |C_j| \le 2 \sum_{k} |c_k|,\]

so the rounding error is about

\[h\,(n+1)\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \cdot 2\sum_k |c_k| = (b-a)(n+1)\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \sum_k |c_k|.\]

These terms are about the size of the whole panel's integral $I$, but near the jump the value needed is about $R$, which can be far smaller. The sum then cancels heavily, and its absolute error is about $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} I$. In the Float32 case of Test 7, the rate $10^8 t^4$ integrates to $I = 2 \times 10^7$ over the step $[0, 1]$, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-7}$, so the rounding error is about 2, against a threshold of order one. Taking the larger of this term and $\varepsilon$ halves the panel until $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} I$ is below the tolerance.

The term is used only on the panel containing the jump. Elsewhere the condition only needs the right sign, and away from the root its value is far from zero, so rounding of this size can't flip it. The panels before the jump contribute only their total $I$, a short weighted sum without cancellation. For the same reason, only that panel gets the deeper halving limit, 40 instead of 12.

Developer notes

These notes are for working on VR_Direct itself.

  • VR_DirectEventCache is both the condition and the affect of the callback. prev_time is $t_0$, the start of the current fit; prev_threshold is $E_{\mathrm{rem}}$ there; current_time is the end of the fitted step; and current_threshold is the last value of $g$ returned.
  • The condition fits a new step when integrator.t != prev_time && (integrator.t != current_time || integrator.tprev != prev_time). This relies on DiffEqBase evaluating continuous conditions only while checking a step, so that (integrator.tprev, integrator.t) identifies it. A step that starts before prev_time is an error.
  • fitted_rate_integral returns a panel's stored end total at or past its end, and never extrapolates. fit_panels! decides whether the threshold was passed with the same number, so the two can't disagree under rounding (Test 12).
  • The state at the fit's points is interpolated into the first of the integrator's temporary caches (get_tmp_cache), and the panel vectors keep their capacity, so fitting and evaluating the condition don't allocate once the vectors have grown.
  • The constants are NUM_CHEB_NODES, MAX_CHEB_DEPTH and MAX_CHEB_CROSSING_DEPTH. The tables, the error estimate and the stored panel size all follow NUM_CHEB_NODES.
  • The tests are Tests 6 to 12 in test/variable_rate.jl, and the test that the callback uses interp_points = 0.
