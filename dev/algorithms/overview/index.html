How jumps are simulated

The pages in this section explain how JumpProcesses simulates jumps, for the aggregators whose methods go beyond the published algorithms they build on. This page covers the machinery they all share. The standard stochastic simulation algorithms (SSAs), such as Direct, NRM and RSSA, follow their references in Jumps, JumpProblem, and Aggregators.

Sampling jump times

Consider jumps $i = 1, \dots, M$ with rates $\lambda_i(u, p, t)$, where $u$ is the state, $p$ the parameters and $t$ the time. Between jumps the state may change continuously, through an ODE or SDE, so a rate can vary in time even while no jump occurs. The total rate is

\[\Lambda(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \lambda_i\big(u(t), p, t\big).\]

The exact methods in JumpProcesses all rest on one fact. If the last jump was at time $t_\ell$, the next jump, of any type, happens at the first time $\tau$ with

\[\int_{t_\ell}^{\tau} \Lambda(s)\, ds = E, \qquad E \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1),\]

and it is jump $i$ with probability $\lambda_i(\tau) / \Lambda(\tau)$. When the rates are constant between jumps, as for ConstantRateJumps and MassActionJumps, the integral is $(\tau - t_\ell) \Lambda$ and $\tau = t_\ell + E / \Lambda$, which is Gillespie's direct method [1].

The jumps can also be treated separately: jump $i$ fires when its own integrated rate, $\int \lambda_i(s)\, ds$, reaches its own exponential threshold $E_i$, and then draws a new one. The thresholds are independent, and the next jump is the first of these times. This gives the same distribution of paths, and is the basis of the next reaction methods [2][3]. Gillespie's first reaction method [1] instead draws new thresholds for all the jumps after each jump, which is equivalent since exponential thresholds are memoryless. Methods built on one threshold for the total rate are called direct methods.

Where each jump is handled

JumpProblem splits the jumps between two algorithms:

  • The aggregator, the positional argument such as Direct(), handles MassActionJumps, ConstantRateJumps and, if it supports them, bounded VariableRateJumps. Currently only Coevolve supports them.
  • The variable-rate aggregator, chosen with the vr_aggregator keyword, handles the other VariableRateJumps: VR_FRM (the default), VR_Direct or VR_DirectFW. It needs a continuous problem, such as an ODEProblem, to step through time.

RegularJumps are for τ-leaping methods, which are not exact and are not covered here.

Both algorithms are callbacks of the time integrator. The aggregator is a DiscreteCallback, and the variable-rate aggregator is one or more ContinuousCallbacks or VectorContinuousCallbacks. The JumpProblem stores them, and every solve initializes them again, drawing new thresholds from the random number generator passed to solve or init.

Aggregators: a discrete callback and stops

An aggregator schedules each jump as a stop of the integrator:

  • When it is initialized, it evaluates the rates, samples the time and type of the next jump, and adds that time to the integrator's tstops, unless it is past the end of the time span.
  • The integrator steps exactly to each of its tstops. At the jump time the aggregator's condition, that the current time is the next jump time, holds, and its affect executes the jump.
  • The affect then updates the rates, samples the next jump, and adds its time to the tstops. Aggregators with a dependency graph recompute only the rates the jump changed.

Since the rates are evaluated only at jumps, they must be constant between jumps. A ConstantRateJump's rate must not depend on time, on components that evolve continuously, or on components that VariableRateJumps change; see the jump diffusion tutorial. For the same reason, a callback that changes u or p must call reset_aggregated_jumps!, so that the aggregator recomputes its rates and next jump.

Coevolve also handles time-varying rates, through bounds. For a bounded VariableRateJump, it samples candidate times from the upper bound urate, which holds over the window given by rateinterval, and accepts a candidate at time $s$ with probability $\lambda(s) / \text{urate}$. This is thinning [4][5]. A rejected candidate, or one past the end of the window, leads to a new candidate. Each candidate is a stop, so the machinery above applies unchanged.

Pure jump problems: SSAStepper

For a JumpProblem over a DiscreteProblem, SSAStepper steps from one stop to the next, at jump times and at any user tstops, with no continuous dynamics to integrate. It runs only DiscreteCallbacks, so it can't be used with general VariableRateJumps.

Coupling to ODE and SDE solvers

With an ODE, SDE, DDE or DAE problem, the solver steps as it normally would. After each step from $t_{\mathrm{prev}}$ to $t$, it checks the callbacks:

  • Continuous callbacks. DiffEqBase evaluates each condition at the step's ends, and also at interp_points points in between on the solver's interpolant. If a condition changes sign, it finds the root on the interpolant. The earliest event among all continuous callbacks cuts the step there, and its affect runs.
  • Discrete callbacks. They are then checked at the end of the step, which may have been cut short. The aggregator's condition holds only if the step ended at its next jump time. Otherwise that time is still a stop, and a later step ends there.

A variable-rate aggregator's condition crosses zero when an integrated rate reaches its threshold, so the jump time is located on the solver's interpolant. Its accuracy depends on how accurately the rates are integrated, which is what the variable-rate aggregators differ in.

Variable-rate aggregators

  • VR_FRM treats each jump separately. It extends the state to an ExtendedJumpArray with one component per jump, which starts at minus the jump's threshold and has the jump's rate as its derivative. The solver integrates these components along with the rest of the state, so its error control covers them, and a component crossing zero is a jump of that type. The rates are evaluated whenever the solver evaluates the right-hand side. Locating jumps with the solver's event detection follows the hybrid method of Salis and Kaznessis [6].
  • VR_Direct and VR_DirectFW use one threshold for the total rate, with one ContinuousCallback, and leave the state as it is. They integrate the total rate over each solver step themselves, then choose which jump fires from the rates at the jump time. VR_DirectFW stores the rate and affect functions in FunctionWrappers, for problems with many jumps. See VRDirect and VRDirectFW.

Dependency graphs

When a jump fires, it usually changes only a few components of the state, and so only a few rates. A dependency graph lists, for each jump, the jumps whose rates it changes, so that an aggregator recomputes only those. NRM, DirectCR, SortingDirect, RSSA, RSSACR and Coevolve use one; see Jump Aggregators Requiring Dependency Graphs. The variable-rate aggregators don't, since the continuous dynamics can change every rate between jumps.

Developer notes

These notes are for working on JumpProcesses itself.

  • JumpProblem builds the aggregator with aggregate and wraps it in a DiscreteCallback (src/aggregators/ssajump.jl). The variable-rate aggregator's callbacks come from configure_jump_problem (src/variable_rate.jl), which for VR_FRM also returns the extended problem.
  • The jump callbacks are stored in the JumpProblem and reused by every solve. Their initialize functions reset all of their state, which is why sequential solves of one problem are independent but concurrent ones are not.
  • All random numbers come from get_rng(integrator), the generator passed to solve or init.
  • In each step, the integrator applies the earliest continuous callback event and then the discrete callbacks (handle_callbacks! in OrdinaryDiffEqCore). DiffEqBase evaluates a continuous callback's condition only while checking a step: at $t_{\mathrm{prev}}$, at $t$, at the interior interp_points, and during root finding. It is never evaluated after an event or between steps. VR_Direct relies on this.
